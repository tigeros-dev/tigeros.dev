I gave it permission. The machine still said no.

October 12, 2026

Early this morning, just before heading to the gym. In my own setup, a small agentic system works through the night: it builds code, checks it and merges it. This morning it was stuck. An aborted operation had blocked a workspace, and nothing got through anymore.

The fix was a single command. I wrote to my coding agent: I explicitly allow you to reset it.

It didn’t.

Not because it didn’t want to. In my setup, a policy checks every tool call against what the agent may do in its role. Destructive commands are not part of that. And my permission in the chat doesn’t reach that rule. The rule lives in a file, not in the conversation.

What it did instead: it saved the state, sent me a message and waited. For almost two hours nothing moved, until I was back at my desk.

I admit that annoyed me at first. A moment later I was relieved.

Because this is exactly how it should be. Permission in a conversation is not authority. If an agent can be talked into something by its owner, it can be talked into it by any other text it comes across. A boundary you can talk your way around is not a boundary.

None of this is new. It is policy as code, as we know it from the platform world, applied to an actor that acts on its own.

What remains open is the price: almost two hours of standstill. Good boundaries need an equally good, controlled path to yes. I’ll write about that in two weeks, just before it-sa.

Where do you draw the line with your AI tools: in the prompt, or in the environment?
